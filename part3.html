Problem 9: Zero Coupon Bonds and Discount Factors

We will go into the term curve and forward rates more deeply in future problems, but intuitively we know that a dollar paid in the future is worth less than a dollar paid today in the case where interest rates are positive. To illustrate this let’s say I can earn continuously compounded interest for two years at rate \(r > 0\) in a bank account. I can back out the value of a dollar paid two years in the future by computing the starting balance, \(B(0)\), today that will yield a dollar two years from now, \(B(2)\). So we have:

\[ B(0) e^{2r} = B(2) = 1 \]

which implies:

\[ B(0) = \frac{1}{e^{2r}} = e^{-2r} \]

\(B(0)\) is today’s value of a dollar paid two years in the future. It is also the price of a \(\textit{Zero Coupon Bond}\) (\(ZCB\)) which is a bond that pays one dollar at maturity, but accrues no interest during its life.

ZCBs are closely related to \(\textit{Discount Factors}\) (\(DFs\)). In general, if I have a function \(r(t)\) that produces continuously compounded discount rates, I can compute the discount factor for the period that starts at time \(t\) and ends at time \(T\) with the following equation:

\[ DF(t, T) = e^{-\int_t^T{r(u)} du}\]

In the special case where \(r(u)\) comes from a forward curve, the ZCB and the DF are equivalent. We will revisit this distinction in future exercises.

NB: \(ZCB(t, T) = \frac{ZCB(0, T)}{ZCB(0, t)}\)

Problem 10: Present Value

Now that we have a way to compute \(ZCBs\) we can use them to compute the time \(t\) value, \(V(t)\), by taking the dot product of the vector of future cash flows with a vector of \(ZCBs\) that correspond to the payment (as opposed to the accrual) dates relative to the time \(t\).

The basic steps are:

  • Use the settle and accrual dates to determine the cash flows the investor bought
  • Use the settle date to determine time t for discounting
  • For each relevant payment date \(T_n\), compute the \(ZCB(t, T_n)\)
  • Take the dot product of your cash flow and \(ZCB\) vectors to compute \(V(t)\)

In the case where \(t>0\) the quantity computed is called a forward value. This is the number of dollars one would buy or sell the instrument at time \(t\).

Create a function that implements the above alogrithm. It might be useful to wrap the required data and algorithms in a class though it isn’t necessary.

Problem 11: Price

In the previous problem, we computed the present value of all future cash flows relative to the trade settle date. Now, we want to focus on how the bond price would be quoted in the market. Ultimately, we aim to calculate the clean price.

To start, we normalize the value by multiplying it by 100 and dividing it by the balance at the settle date. This gives us the dirty price. In other words:

\[ \text{Dirty Price} = \text{Value} \times \frac{100}{\text{Balance at Settle}} \]

For a detailed explanation of the terms dirty price and clean price, refer to the following resources:

  • Dirty Price (Wikipedia)
  • Clean Price (Wikipedia)

Both articles mention the concept of accrued interest. Before we compute accrued interest for our bond, we need to understand day count conventions. You can read more about them here:

  • Day Count Convention (Wikipedia)

Our bond uses the 30/360 day count method. We need to compute the interest accrued from the partial period that runs from the first of the trade settle month to the trade settle date. Excel has a function called DAYS360 that is useful for computing this quantity.

Task: Write a function that computes the simplest 30/360 method described on Wikipedia (Bond Basis) and use it in another function to convert a (dirty) value to a clean price. It is acceptable to report the dirty price and accrued interest as well.

Problem 12: Option Adjusted Spread

The prices generated using the logic above often (if not always) differ from the market-observed prices of MBS. To reconcile this difference, an Option Adjusted Spread (OAS) is added to the discount rates.

To keep things easier let’s work with values instead of prices. Given a discount date grid, discount rate grid, settlement date, cash flows, and a target value, write a function to compute the OAS that aligns the computed price with the target value.

NB: This is only involves discounting. It doesn’t change your cash flows. The OAS is a constant spread applied to the continuously compounded discount rates. Since the spreads may vary across securities, it’s advisable not to adjust the discount rates directly.

NB: I am ignoring a technical issue for forward settles here. My forward value equation is:

\[ V(t, OAS) = \frac{V(0, OAS)}{ZCB(t, OAS)}\]

instead of

\[ V(t, OAS) = \frac{V(0, OAS)}{ZCB(t, 0.0)}\]
