Problem 21: Monte Carlo Paths via Hull-White

To generate Monte Carlo paths of rates we use the classic Hull-White model for short rates.

The One-Factor Hull-White Model

The one-factor Hull-White model describes the evolution of short-term interest rates \(r(t)\) via the stochastic differential equation (SDE):

\[dr(t) = [\theta(t) - \alpha r(t)]\, dt + \sigma\, dW(t)\]

Key components:

  1. Mean Reversion: The term \(-\alpha r(t)\) pulls \(r(t)\) toward a time-dependent mean. \(\alpha > 0\) is the mean reversion speed.
  2. Volatility: \(\sigma\) is the volatility of the short rate, scaled by Brownian motion \(W(t)\).
  3. Time-Dependent Drift \(\theta(t)\): Ensures the model fits the initial term structure of interest rates.

Discretization

Using the Euler-Maruyama scheme:

\[r_{t+\Delta t} = r_t + (\theta(t) - \alpha r_t)\,\Delta t + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_t\]

where:

  • \(r_t\): Short rate from your forward curve at time \(t\)
  • \(\Delta t\): Time step size
  • \(Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\): Standard normal draw

To compute \(\theta(t_i)\):

\[\theta(t_i) = \frac{f_{t_{i+1}} - f_{t_i}}{\Delta t} + \alpha f_{t_i} + \frac{\sigma^2}{2\alpha}\left(1 - e^{-2\alpha t_i}\right)\]

where \(f_t\) is the continuously compounded forward rate from your forward curve. Choose your grid so that \(t\) always hits a knot point on your step discount rate function — no interpolation required.

Stochastic Discount Factors

Each Monte Carlo path produces a rate function that can be used to discount future cash flows. Our discount factors are now stochastic (SDF). The SDFs are not zero coupon bonds. However, the average of pathwise SDFs equals the corresponding ZCB:

\[ZCB(t, T) = \mathbb{E}[\text{SDF}(t, T)] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-\int_t^T r(u)\,du}\right]\]

Steps for Simulation

  1. Initialize: Set the starting short rate \(r_0\) from the initial rate of your forward curve.
  2. Iterate: For each time step, compute \(r_{t+\Delta t}\) using the discretized equation.
  3. Simulate paths: Repeat across multiple paths by drawing independent \(Z_t\) for each.
  4. Aggregate: Use the simulated paths to compute expectations, price derivatives, or analyze rate distributions.

NB: Smaller \(\Delta t\) improves accuracy but increases computational cost. Variance reduction techniques are explored in Problem 23.

Problem 23: Antithetic Variates

Looking at the results above, you may notice that the variances can be quite high and Monte Carlo values are a bit off from the forward curve results. Antithetic variates is a simple and elegant variance reduction technique. If we ask for 100 paths, we draw 50 and add 50 more that are the negatives of the original 50.

For example, if the first path of draws from the standard normal is:

.5, -.25, .01, .02, ...

we negate each entry to get:

-.5, .25, -.01, -.02, ...

Update your code, rerun the securities with antithetics, and compare results. Use the correct equation for antithetic variance.
